# Four-goal client acquisition plan

Planning only. No campaign, site change, prospect contact or pricing change is authorised by this draft. Goal is paying clients and sustainable delivery; revenue target, available time, current leads and conversion baseline await founder input. No conversion forecast is established.

## Evidence and assumptions
Read wiki/projects/maz-works/STATUS.md and CALL-DESK.md. Retrieved live https://mazworks.uk on 2026-09-18. Live site lists websites, automation, AI/software and physical Objects; Quick Win £150, Website Launch from £299, Growth System from £499, optional support from £49/month. Free near-working demos are prominent. Scrap Finance Partners is described as contract client work; JobFilter is a product. Neither is evidence of measured client revenue gains. Current client counts and acquisition performance are unknown. Objects physical readiness remains unverified in the available status note. Unified Memory project maz-works returned unknown_project.

Working hypothesis: one commercially specific digital-service offer, using relevant existing proof, is the first acquisition test. Do not assume an industry until the founder confirms access, demand and delivery fit. Narrowing is a campaign choice; Maz Works can retain its broader identity.

## Decision method
Consider buyer urgency, ability to pay, access to decision makers, strength of relevant proof, time to first paid outcome, delivery effort and repeatability. Evaluate from buyer, sales, operator and financial perspectives. Rank offers qualitatively using evidence; do not invent market sizes, conversion rates or ROI. Compare website enquiry fixes, follow-up automation and Objects against these criteria after founder context. Prioritise existing warm demand over fresh cold acquisition if present.

## Goal 1 — Make one offer easy to understand and buy
Choose one reachable buyer segment, one costly problem, one bounded paid result and one primary next step. Draft a short offer brief and the minimum headline/CTA/pricing explanation needed for its acquisition path. Explain what the buyer gets, scope exclusions, completion test and handover. Compare the existing £150 entry offer with realistic delivery effort and a larger scoped offer; do not change prices without evidence. Example direction, not approved copy: help service businesses make website enquiries easier to capture and follow up.
Acceptance: a prospect can identify fit, deliverable, price basis and next step; delivery hours and third-party costs are estimated; offer tested in actual buyer conversations before broad rollout.
Dependency: founder context. First bounded work package: offer brief and up to three wording alternatives.

## Goal 2 — Give buyers relevant reasons to trust the offer
Create one strong, accurate case study from existing client work with permission for any non-public material, a short reusable walkthrough and three proof-led content pieces derived from it. Show the starting problem, delivered change, screenshots and measured outcome if verified. Label demonstrations as demonstrations and products as products. If outcome data is unavailable, show verified delivered behaviour without inventing commercial impact. Seek a testimonial/referral only as a proposed founder action until contact is explicitly authorised.
Acceptance: every claim has evidence; the proof answers the chosen buyer's main objection; each piece leads to the same offer and has a defined audience/distribution route.
Dependency: Goal 1. First bounded work package: evidence inventory and case-study outline. Avoid repeated free bespoke demos; qualify fit first and timebox any custom demonstration.

## Goal 3 — Create a repeatable source of qualified conversations
Start with past clients, existing enquiries, introductions and complementary partners where these exist. Then test one focused acquisition channel for the chosen segment. Use existing LeadFinder; retain applicable Call Desk screening controls rather than building another CRM. Prepare a small, manually reviewed batch (initial proposal: 20 relevant prospects), a real observation for each, an honest opener and a next-action record. Founder selects/authorises actual outreach separately. Follow the existing screening and suppression workflow; recheck current authoritative rules before executing any new outreach method.
Acceptance: each prospect fits the segment and has a relevant need signal; each conversation has an outcome and next date; report qualified conversations and opportunities by source. Batch size is a learning limit, not a conversion forecast.
Dependency: minimum usable offer and proof. First bounded work package: channel selection, targeting rules and outreach drafts. Expand volume only after inspecting responses.

## Goal 4 — Convert interest into profitable paid work and referrals
Prepare discovery questions, qualification criteria, a concise scoped proposal, agreed payment milestones, follow-up dates and a handover/proof request. Track conversation → qualified opportunity → proposal → paid client, cash collected and delivery hours. Define a qualified opportunity as a relevant problem with an identifiable decision maker, plausible budget and intended timing. After each paid delivery, request permission to document the result and ask for a relevant introduction.
Acceptance: no warm opportunity lacks a dated next action; each proposal has scope, price, completion criteria and payment terms; delivered jobs have actual time/cost recorded. Founder sets revenue and capacity target before interpreting client-count success.
Dependency: Goal 1 for proposal template; live validation follows Goal 3. First bounded work package: proposal/follow-up kit and simple scorecard.

## Sequence and review gates
1. Confirm buyer, traction, target and available sales/delivery time; choose offer.
2. Produce only enough proof and conversion material to support a small test.
3. Run founder-authorised outreach test; classify responses and objections.
4. Diagnose before spending further: no conversations suggests access/message issues; conversations without real need suggests targeting; qualified interest without a sale suggests trust, scope, timing or price; sales with poor economics require delivery/scope changes.
5. Scale only the route that produces paid work at acceptable effort. Content continues where it supports conversations and proof.

Defer broad rebranding, a new acquisition application, daily generic content, a multi-channel campaign and bespoke unpaid builds for unqualified leads during this first test. Reconsider if evidence shows these solve the actual bottleneck. Do not expand Objects acquisition until product readiness and economics are verified.

Four concrete deliverables:
1. One acquisition offer page/brief: buyer, visible enquiry/follow-up problem, fixed-scope paid outcome, existing price basis, one next step; at most three message variants. No site-wide redesign.
2. One reusable sales-proof package: 60–90 second workflow walkthrough, concise case study and three derivative posts explaining specific buyer problems. Use delivered behaviour and explicitly separate unmeasured business outcomes. Content serves the selected outreach test.
3. One reviewed 20-business acquisition experiment in LeadFinder: warm relationships first; otherwise one segment/channel, a specific observation per prospect and authorised manual outreach with dated next actions. Evaluate qualified conversations, not lead-list size.
4. One conversion and referral process: discovery, scoped proposal/deposit terms, bounded follow-up, paid delivery baseline, permission-based testimonial/referral request and a weekly source-to-payment scorecard. Measure sales and delivery hours as well as cash collected.

Objects is a challenger acquisition route: a tested physical stand can open a local-business conversation leading to website or workflow work. Only test this after physical/NFC/QR validation and margin/time checks. Do not assume a £29–£79 object sale produces a profitable software upsell. Existing relevant prototype work may lower the test cost, but it is not published outcome proof.

Initial horizon: one two-week learning cycle, paced to founder availability. Objective is identify a route to profitable paid clients. Counts are experiment bounds; no sales yield or revenue promise. Start minimal conversations as soon as usable proof exists rather than waiting for all content. Report bottleneck and choose next action before expanding campaign work.
