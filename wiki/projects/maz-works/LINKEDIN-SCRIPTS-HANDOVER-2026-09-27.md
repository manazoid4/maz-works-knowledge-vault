## What needs improvement

1. Strengthen the first two spoken lines. Use an immediately recognisable event, cost or trust consequence. Avoid generic "Do you need a website?" introductions.
2. Show the mechanism: owner situation → customer experience → plausible commercial consequence → useful change → next action. Vary the delivery so ten posts do not sound like the same template.
3. Reduce overlap. Drafts 1, 3 and 5 all involve failed enquiries; drafts 2 and 8 overlap on first impressions. Merge, replace or differentiate these deliberately.
4. Show more of Maz's wider value. Include a meaningful mix of booking/enquiry repair, follow-up, repeated admin, customer updates and internal information handoffs.
5. Make the solution tangible. Say what Maz might change and what the customer/owner can do afterwards. "Make things simpler" alone is too vague.
6. Balance the loss with a desirable outcome: confident customers, easier booking, a clear next step, less chasing or owner time recovered. Avoid ten consecutive fear-based posts.
7. Improve engagement with a specific, easy-to-answer question when discussion is the main purpose. Avoid generic "thoughts?", engagement bait and demanding a comment, DM, call and form in the same post.
8. Give each post one primary action. Use the free check for website/booking enquiries, the call for wider process problems, and a relevant question for selected discussion posts.
9. Build a series with progression and varied openings. Do not repeat "Through Maz Works", "That's where I help" or an identical sales paragraph in every post.
10. Keep the voice conversational, credible and easy to say aloud. Use British English. No jargon, inflated authority, cheesy pressure, fake anecdotes or robotic one-sentence fragments throughout.

## Numbers, proof and filming

- Hypothetical money examples must be clearly labelled inside the script. For example: £80 × 2 bookings × 4 weeks = £640 potential booking revenue.
- Potential sales are not profit, verified losses or guaranteed recoverable income. Do not imply that a £395 repair necessarily earns its cost back.
- Time saved is capacity or personal time, not automatically cash saved. Separate these.
- Do not claim every lost lead is caused by the website, or that better follow-up makes every prospect buy.
- Demonstrations can be labelled "Example" or "Demo". No invented inbox evidence, customers, before/after results or footage.
- If proposing a real example, identify the evidence and permission needed. Offer a filmable illustrative alternative so the content does not depend on unavailable client proof.
- Do not add unsupported statements about LinkedIn algorithms, ideal posting times or links suppressing reach. Keeping links in first comments is Maz's formatting preference.

## Required delivery format

First give a short editorial diagnosis, maximum five bullets, explaining the most important improvements.

Then deliver exactly ten final posts. For each include:
1. A short working title, intended business outcome and primary CTA.
2. The complete spoken script in its own plain code block. Aim for roughly 80–120 words and a natural 35–60 second delivery; adjust for the actual read.
3. One simple filming idea and a short opening on-screen line, outside the script.
4. A ready-to-paste LinkedIn caption in a separate plain code block. Keep it concise, useful without watching and complementary to the video. No placeholders.
5. The complete first comment in its own code block where a link is needed. Do not make Maz assemble fragments or find the link elsewhere.

Use short paragraphs and normal punctuation. No em or en dashes in publication copy. No markdown formatting inside the post text. At most three relevant hashtags; omit if unnecessary. Keep LinkedIn copy below 3,000 characters.

Recommend a publishing order and explain it briefly. Do not invent optimal days or times. State what to observe: relevant comments, available watch/click metrics, check requests, booked calls and deposits. No claimed baseline or guaranteed target.

Finish with a concise claim audit identifying any outstanding fact or evidence needed. Prefer writing around missing evidence so the scripts are usable now.

## Useful themes from earlier iterations

The conversation produced earlier versions before the latest ten. These themes were valued and can replace repetitive drafts. They are optional source ideas, not an instruction to produce extra posts.

- Quote follow-up: interested prospects may still have a question or need a timely follow-up. Show a visible next action and reminder without promising a sale.
- Repeated admin: 30 minutes per working day × 5 days × 4 weeks = 10 hours. Show the repeated task and an achievable simpler flow. Ten hours is not automatically a cash saving.
- Return customers: illustrate easier rebooking or opted-in reminders. Four additional £40 visits would equal £160 in sales, not profit. Keep the example hypothetical.
- Empty appointments: a £60 unfilled slot once a week represents £240 potential revenue over four weeks. Discuss confirmation, timely cancellation and rearranging without promising a full diary.
- Owner bottleneck: quotes, replies or invoices wait because information lives in one person's head. Show how a shared record and next action can help a team work without repeated interruptions.
- Making the offer concrete: free check first; a scoped £395 Repair only where appropriate; other software/automation quoted separately; no compulsory call for the check.
