```
One letter was costing a local dessert shop.

Their website title, the line Google shows in search results, spelled "Dessert" as "Desert".

It sounds tiny. But that line is often the first thing a hungry customer reads at 11pm, deciding where to go.

The fix took minutes:

→ add a one-tap "leave us a review" link

This is what a £150 Quick Win looks like: one clear change, fixed price, done fast.

Small businesses rarely have one big problem. They have lots of tiny ones nobody has time to spot.
```

### POST 3 — MEDIUM: the site that won't open (after contacting Yumi)
```
A well-loved ice cream parlour's website won't open securely.

On most phones that means a warning screen, or nothing at all.

The owner probably has no idea. The site worked when it was built. Then a security certificate or server setting quietly expired.

Every day it stays broken, people who searched for them give up and go somewhere else.

What I check first when a site "just stopped working":
→ is the security certificate valid?
→ does the plain (http) address redirect properly?
→ does it load on a phone on 4G?

Most of these are same-day fixes. The hard part is someone noticing.

If you're not sure your site opens properly, send me the link. I'll check it for free.
```

### POST 4 — MEDIUM: no way to book (after Homemade Cafe or similar)
```
"Please note, we do not accept reservations via this form."

That line was on a lovely park café's contact page.

So how do you book? You don't. You call, maybe, if you can find the number. On a phone there wasn't a tap-to-call button either.

Three fixes, smallest first:
1. Tap-to-call button at the top on mobile
2. One clear "how to book" line
3. A tap stand on the counter that opens the review page

None of this is a new website. It's removing the reasons people give up.
```
